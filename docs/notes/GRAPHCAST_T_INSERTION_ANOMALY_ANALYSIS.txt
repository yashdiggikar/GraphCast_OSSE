0. WHY THIS REPORT EXISTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This project's own 60-day OSSE truth-insertion study (control/ + osse_z_global/
+ osse_t_global/ + osse_uv_global/, see session_log.md for full provenance)
found that hard-replacing temperature with ERA5 truth at all 37 native
pressure levels, every 6 hours, from day 30 through day 60, catastrophically
DEGRADES GraphCast's geopotential and wind fields relative to a free-running
control -- rather than improving them, which is what the same experiment does
in every one of the other three AI weather models this study is designed to
be compared against. This report documents that finding in detail, verifies
it is not a data-pipeline artifact, and explains it using GraphCast's
published architecture, the quasi-geostrophic dynamical-balance literature,
and independent supporting evidence already established in this research
program's earlier GraphCast-Small study.


1. EXPERIMENT DESIGN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each of the four models is run as a 60-day (240-step at 6-hourly cadence for
GraphCast/Prithvi; 120-step at 12-hourly cadence for Aurora/FCN) free-running
forecast from a January-2022 (GraphCast/Prithvi) or February-2023
(Aurora/FCN) initial condition (Control), then re-run with a single
dynamical field hard-replaced ("inserted") by its ERA5 (or MERRA-2, for
Prithvi) truth value at every step from day 30 onward: temperature (T-Ins),
wind U+V (Wind-Ins/UV-Ins), or geopotential/height (Geo-Ins/Z-Ins). No
blending or damping is applied in any of the four models' insertion pipeline
-- this is a hard, full-field overwrite in every case, confirmed directly in
this project's own graphcast/rollout.py and, for the other three models, in
fcn_inference/inf.py (which sets sfno_model.truth_ds directly) and the shared
OSSE design documented in session_log.md Section 12.

For this project's GraphCast-full runs specifically: truth is inserted at
ALL 37 of the checkpoint's native pressure levels (not just the 13 levels
that get subset out at save time), starting at 0-based step 120
(lead = 726h = day 30.25), through step 239 (lead = 1440h = day 60), for
temperature, geopotential, and u+v wind, each as its own independent
experiment (osse_t_global/, osse_z_global/, osse_uv_global/), against the
same free-running control/ baseline.

The metric used throughout is the standard cross-variable "coupling" /
"percent RMSE improvement" score:
    pct_improvement(B) = 100 x (RMSE_control(B) - RMSE_insertion(B)) / RMSE_control(B)
averaged over the mature phase (day 30-60) and, for the cross-model
comparison table below, over pressure level -- positive = insertion made the
non-inserted field B more accurate than the free-running control; negative =
insertion made it LESS accurate.


2. METHODOLOGICAL NOTE: A REAL BUG WAS FOUND AND FIXED BEFORE THESE NUMBERS
   WERE PRODUCED -- THE ANOMALY SURVIVED THE FIX AND GOT STRONGER, NOT WEAKER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Before trusting this result, a genuine implementation bug was found (by
direct suspicion that the injected variable's own post-insertion RMSE should
drop to ~0 and stay there, since it is literally ERA5 truth, but instead
showed a period-4 sawtooth). Root cause: chunked_prediction_generator's
internal JIT-recompilation-avoidance trick relabels each chunk's predictions
with a fixed placeholder time coordinate (always chunk 0's lead times); the
insertion code's spatial-alignment step (`reindex_like(..., method="nearest")`)
was, as an unintended side effect, ALSO reindexing on that fake time
coordinate, collapsing 3 of every 4 injected sub-steps onto a single stale
truth value instead of their own. Fixed (pin truth's time coordinate to the
prediction's before reindexing, in all three graphcast/rollout.py copies);
reverified directly against the corrected output (0.0 mean abs diff between
prediction and truth at every post-injection sub-step, not just 1-in-4).

Critically for this report: fixing the bug made the T-insertion anomaly
WORSE, not better. Under the bug, only 1-in-4 insertion events actually
delivered the full "hard replace" shock (the other 3 got a smoother, stale
repeat); post-fix, every single 6-hourly step delivers the full shock. The
corrected geopotential degradation is -70.9% to -476.0% (mean -199.6%)
across levels, versus -110% to -344% at its worst single level under the
buggy run. This is strong internal evidence that the anomaly is a genuine
response of the model to correctly-delivered, physically discontinuous
forcing -- not an artifact of the (now-fixed) implementation defect.

4. CONFOUNDS RULED OUT                                             [ESTABLISHED]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 Not the injection bug. Section 2 above; the anomaly survives and
    strengthens after the fix, verified against directly-checked output.

4.2 Not insertion cadence. Aurora and FCN insert every 12h (120 events over
    the 60-day run); GraphCast and Prithvi insert every 6h (240 events) --
    twice as often. If frequency alone explained GraphCast's blowup, Prithvi
    (identical 6h cadence) should show the same pathology. It does not
    (T-Ins -> U/V/H all positive, Section 3). Cadence is not the driver.

4.3 Not insertion mechanism. All four models use the same hard-replace,
    no-blending design -- confirmed directly in this project's own
    graphcast/rollout.py and in fcn_inference/inf.py (which sets
    sfno_model.truth_ds directly, no damping parameter), and documented as
    the shared OSSE convention across CC_aurora/, prithviOSSE/,
    fcn_inference/, and this project (session_log.md Section 12). This is a
    genuinely apples-to-apples comparison of insertion STYLE; only the
    receiving model differs.

4.4 Not a crash, NaN, or discontinuity artifact. Directly inspected Z500
    field values at the injection boundary versus at the end of the run
    (session_log.md Section 14): at chunk_0030 (steps 121-124, the first
    insertion chunk), T-insertion's Z500 (mean 53722 m^2/s^2, range
    46878-58299) is nearly identical to control's (mean 53714, range
    46796-58267) -- both physically sane. By chunk_0059 (steps 237-240, the
    final chunk), T-insertion's Z500 has drifted far outside the
    climatological range (mean 60175, range 35643-79021) while control
    remains sane (mean 54068, range 42525-59987). No NaN/Inf anywhere. This
    is smooth, monotonic-looking divergence over ~30 days of continued
    insertion, not a numerical failure -- consistent with an accumulating
    physical instability, not a bug.


5. CANDIDATE EXPLANATIONS FOR THE [ESTABLISHED] FINDING            [HYPOTHESIS]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IMPORTANT: everything in this section is [HYPOTHESIS] or, for 5.3,
[NEEDS FOLLOW-UP] -- see the epistemic-status legend at the top of this
report. Aurora, FCN, Prithvi, and GraphCast are four different systems that
differ simultaneously in architecture, training data/truth source (ERA5 for
three, MERRA-2 for Prithvi), horizontal resolution, native vertical-level
count, and initial-condition date/season (Section 9). This section proposes
three physically plausible, literature-consistent factors that COULD explain
the Section 3 contrast, in decreasing order of how well-supported each one
currently is -- but none of them has been isolated by a controlled
experiment that holds the other factors fixed. Do not present this section
in a manuscript as "the explanation" without the hedging language used
throughout it, and without running (or explicitly flagging as future work)
the Section 8 experiments that would test each one directly.

5.1 [HYPOTHESIS] Local message-passing versus global receptive field per
    forward step.

GraphCast's encode-process-decode graph neural network (Lam et al., 2023,
Science / arXiv:2212.12794) represents the sphere via a fixed icosahedral
multi-mesh and propagates information through a fixed number (16) of
message-passing rounds, each of which moves information exactly one
mesh-edge hop. A single 6-hour forward pass therefore has a genuinely LOCAL,
finite spatial receptive field -- information from one grid point cannot,
within a single step, influence points more than ~16 mesh-edges away.

The other three models all have architecturally much larger, often-global
receptive fields per single forward pass:
  - FourCastNet v2 (Pathak et al., 2022, arXiv:2202.11214) is a spherical
    Fourier neural operator: its core operation is a spherical-harmonic
    transform, a global, spectrally band-limited representation. Any
    small-scale discontinuity introduced by hard insertion is immediately
    projected onto global basis functions, and truncation of high
    wavenumbers acts as an implicit low-pass filter -- shock content is
    damped on the very first forward pass, before it can compound
    autoregressively. This is consistent with FCN showing the SMALLEST
    positive T-Ins coupling of the three well-behaved models (+18.0% U,
    +16.0% V) -- strong smoothing damps both the harm GraphCast shows and
    the benefit Aurora/Prithvi extract.
  - Aurora (Bodnar et al., 2024/2025, Nature / arXiv:2405.13063) uses a
    Swin-Transformer windowed-attention backbone with a 3D Perceiver-style
    encoder/decoder; windowed attention (with shifting) achieves a much
    larger effective receptive field per step than a fixed 16-hop mesh, and
    the model's rollout-aware, multi-source foundation-model training gives
    it the smoothest, most terrain-consistent cross-variable response of
    any of the four models in this whole research program (see Section 6).
  - Prithvi WxC (Schmude et al., 2024, arXiv:2409.13598) is a patch-embedded
    Vision Transformer pretrained with a masked-reconstruction-plus-
    forecasting objective. Masked-autoencoder pretraining specifically
    trains the network to infer plausible values for one patch/variable
    FROM the surrounding spatial and cross-variable context -- an objective
    that inherently teaches a soft, denoising-like consistency behavior
    across co-located fields, which plausibly explains why Prithvi's
    coupling stays weakly positive (never catastrophically negative) even
    though its own wind pathway is independently documented (Organized_results
    common/lat_band/PHYSICAL_ANALYSIS.txt Section 1.4) as its weakest
    learned balance relation of the four models.

GraphCast alone has neither a global spectral operator nor an
attention-based averaging mechanism. A temperature discontinuity introduced
simultaneously at every grid point and all 37 levels is exactly the kind of
input its local, convolution-like operators have no architectural means to
damp within a single step.

Caveat: this is an architectural plausibility argument, not a demonstrated
causal mechanism. No experiment in this report varies receptive-field size
while holding everything else (training data, resolution, vertical levels)
fixed -- the four models simply happen to differ this way. A rigorous test
would require, e.g., an ablated GraphCast variant with a wider message-
passing radius, which has not been run.

5.2 [HYPOTHESIS, WITH INDEPENDENT SUPPORTING EVIDENCE] No learned
    geostrophic-adjustment mechanism -- independently documented in this
    research program's own earlier GraphCast-Small study.

This is not a new speculation; it is a previously-established, independent
finding from a DIFFERENT GraphCast configuration (1 degree, thesis Ch.5
study, Organized_results/GraphCast/ and common/lat_band/PHYSICAL_ANALYSIS.txt
-- cited here only as prior supporting context, not as source data for the
numbers in Section 3). That report found, under wind insertion at the
equator, GraphCast-Small's T500 response was -50.2% (more than doubling
error) at 2 deg N versus +58.9% at 40 deg N -- "the same model that benefits
enormously from wind information at the jet-stream latitudes is actively
damaged by the same kind of information at the equator," and concluded the
model "has no mechanism to safely 'ignore' or down-weight" information that
is inconsistent with the balance relationship it has learned, unlike a true
dynamical system, in which a locally-forced imbalance should radiate away as
inertia-gravity waves (the classical Rossby/Charney geostrophic-adjustment
problem; Charney, 1947/1948) and leave the slow, balanced flow largely
unaffected.

The same report's cloud-regime companion analysis
(Organized_results/common/cloud/PHYSICAL_ANALYSIS_T_Ins.txt Section 1.4)
independently found, for GraphCast-Small's T-insertion specifically, a
TEMPORAL sign reversal: Z500's response is positive in every cloud regime at
the Adjustment phase (day 30-40) but goes negative in three of four regimes
by the Mature phase (day 40-60) -- "the insertion helps GraphCast's
geopotential field in the short term and then actively degrades it after
continued exposure ... GraphCast's short-term gains from insertion are not
dynamically 'locked in' by its own forward evolution, and tend to decay or
reverse the longer the forecast continues."

Both of these are independent, previously-existing observations of the same
qualitative signature -- gains from forced information are not durable,
because the model has no mechanism to reconcile information that violates
its learned balance -- from a DIFFERENT GraphCast checkpoint/resolution
under a DIFFERENT insertion experiment (wind, not temperature). Their
recurrence here, now at full severity under 37-level global temperature
insertion in the full-resolution checkpoint, is consistent with (not proof
of) this being a genuine, reproducible property of GraphCast's architecture
and training, rather than a one-off artifact of this particular run.

Caveat, UPDATED after running Section 8.1 (see Section 9 for full detail):
this is now the best-supported of the three factors for Z-INSERTION and
UV-INSERTION specifically -- Section 9.2 reproduces the exact equator-to-
pole breakdown signature IN-STUDY, in this same full-resolution checkpoint,
under two different insertion experiments, not just by analogy to a
different GraphCast configuration. That is a materially stronger standard
of evidence than existed when this paragraph was first written. However,
Section 9.1 shows this specific Rossby-number mechanism does NOT explain
the T-INSERTION failure this report is centrally about -- T-Ins degrades
every latitude slice tested (2N through 60N), including deep extratropics
where Z-Ins/UV-Ins recover fully. So: "no learned adjustment mechanism" as
a general architectural property is now better-supported than before: for
T-insertion specifically, "purely a Rossby-number/tropical phenomenon" is
RULED OUT by 9.1, and a broader-than-tropics failure mode is implicated
instead (see Section 9.3's reconciliation and Section 6's "central
asymmetry" argument, which predicts T-to-wind/mass inversion should be hard
at every latitude, not just where f -> 0).

5.3 [NEEDS FOLLOW-UP -- CURRENTLY AN UNRESOLVED ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATION, NOT
    A CONFIRMED CONTRIBUTING FACTOR] Vertical resolution: 37 levels versus
    13-14.

This is a checkable configuration fact, not inference. Aurora's and
FourCastNet v2's coupling pipelines both operate on the standard 13-level
set (LEVELS = [1000, 925, 850, 700, 600, 500, 400, 300, 250, 200, 150, 100,
50], confirmed directly in Organized_results/Aurora/coupling/scripts/
compute_aurora_coupling.py and .../FourCastNet/coupling/scripts/
compute_coupling.py); Prithvi WxC uses 14 native MERRA-2 levels
(Organized_results/map.md Section 3.5). Full GraphCast -- the entire
distinguishing feature of the "full" checkpoint over "small"/"operational"
variants -- runs at 37 native pressure levels, and this project's insertion
hard-replaces truth at all 37 simultaneously, every 6 hours (confirmed in
this directory's checkpoint inspection, session_log.md Section 3). That is
roughly 2.6-3x the vertical degrees of freedom being forced at once compared
to what the other three models' own T-insertion experiments perturb. More
vertical DOF forced abruptly creates more opportunity for a locally
unphysical lapse-rate/thickness profile between adjacent levels -- exactly
the kind of high-vertical-wavenumber content a local, non-spectral
architecture (Section 5.1) has no mechanism to filter, compounding across
120 consecutive 6-hourly insertion steps.

Caveat -- read this before citing 5.3 as a "contributing factor": as
currently written, this is presented as compounding with 5.1/5.2, but it is
equally possible that vertical resolution is doing most or all of the work
by itself, independent of anything architectural -- i.e. that hard-replacing
37 levels of ANY model (Aurora, FCN, Prithvi included, if they had a
37-level checkpoint) would produce the same catastrophic degradation, which
would mean the headline finding is really "forcing more vertical DOF than a
model can reconcile is destabilizing," not "GraphCast's architecture is
uniquely fragile." These two explanations make different, testable
predictions and have NOT been distinguished by any experiment in this
report. The ablation in Section 8.3 (hard-replace only 13 of GraphCast's 37
levels, holding architecture fixed) is the direct test: if the blowup
persists, 5.3 is ruled OUT as the primary driver and 5.1/5.2 stand on their
own; if the blowup is substantially smaller, 5.3 is at least a major
contributing factor and the "GraphCast's architecture is uniquely fragile"
framing needs to be softened to "GraphCast's architecture is uniquely
fragile TO the higher-vertical-DOF forcing its full checkpoint invites."
Until this ablation is run, treat 5.3 as an open, competing hypothesis, not
a settled supporting factor.


6. WHY TEMPERATURE SPECIFICALLY, NOT GEOPOTENTIAL OR WIND               [HYPOTHESIS]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
GraphCast full's own Z-insertion and UV-insertion results (Section 3) are
mild and mostly positive -- qualitatively like every other model's numbers.
The failure is specific to the T-insertion DIRECTION, and this is exactly
what quasi-geostrophic theory predicts (already established as the "central
asymmetry" in this program's earlier work,
Organized_results/common/lat_band/PHYSICAL_ANALYSIS.txt Section 1.1):
recovering the mass/wind field FROM a temperature anomaly is the harder,
ill-posed inverse problem -- in the continuous case it requires
differentiating a (noisy, discretely sampled) thermal field and solving a
global elliptic (Poisson-type) inversion for the rotational wind, a
computation sensitive to boundary conditions and to exactly the part of the
flow (the divergent/ageostrophic component) that a pure balance relation
cannot supply. Recovering mass FROM wind, by contrast, is a local, algebraic
diagnostic (f*v = (1/rho) dp/dx) -- which is why Wind/UV-insertion is the
easy, robust direction for every model in this whole four-model program,
GraphCast full included (its own UV-insertion -> Z coupling is +52.5% mean,
in line with the other three models' T-Ins-> Z numbers). Every model
attempts the same hard T-to-wind/mass inversion under T-insertion; only
GraphCast lacks any of the three mitigating properties in Section 5 to keep
that harder inversion's errors from compounding into an outright divergence
rather than merely a smaller gain.

T-Ins, in the same latitude band where Z-Ins and UV-Ins recover to +50% to

an artifact of comparing different studies. It suggests T-insertion is
triggering something beyond (or in addition to) the equatorial
geostrophic-breakdown mechanism -- consistent with, and now more concretely
motivating, Section 6's "central asymmetry" argument (recovering wind/mass
FROM temperature is the fundamentally harder, ill-posed inversion direction,
for every model, at every latitude) plus Section 5.3's still-unresolved
vertical-DOF question. A plausible refined reading: the geostrophic/
thermal-wind relation Z-Ins and UV-Ins rely on to reconstruct the OTHER

has to survive the harder T-to-wind/mass elliptic inversion (Section 6) AT
EVERY latitude, and GraphCast's architecture (Section 5.1) apparently cannot
do that reliably anywhere, not even where its OWN geostrophic-adjustment
pathway (as demonstrated by 9.2) works fine for the other two insertion
directions. This remains a [HYPOTHESIS] for the underlying mechanism, but
9.1-9.2's pattern itself -- global T-Ins failure vs. latitude-organised
Z-Ins/UV-Ins failure, within the same model and dataset -- is now
[ESTABLISHED].

10. PERTURBED-TRUTH ROBUSTNESS CHECK: DOES REALISTIC OBSERVATION ERROR CHANGE
    THE FINDING? (COMPLETED 2026-08-24)                              [ESTABLISHED]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.0 Motivation and method.

Every insertion experiment in this report so far (Sections 3, 9) hard-replaces
GraphCast's state with EXACT ERA5 truth -- no observation error. A natural
objection: maybe the T-insertion instability is partly an artifact of forcing
the model with an unrealistically perfect, noise-free signal, and would look
different under a more realistic OSSE design where "truth" carries
observation-error-like noise, the way real assimilated observations do.

Tested this directly by porting the "Correlated OSSE Perturbation" (COP)
scheme already implemented for this research program's other three models
(Aurora, FourCastNet v2, Prithvi WxC -- see
/umbc/rs/pi_halem/users/yashd1/PERTURBATION_EXPERIMENTS/METHOD_OVERVIEW.md for
the full cross-model method writeup and citations: Errico et al. 2013,
Ingleby 2017, Hollingsworth & Lonnberg 1986, Bormann & Bauer 2010, Bormann et
al. 2016, Bannister 2008, Parrish & Derber 1992, Holton) to full GraphCast.
Two new experiments, T_PERTURBED_CORR/ and UV_PERTURBED_CORR/ (same
directory-per-experiment convention as the rest of this project; see
PERTURBATION_IMPLEMENTATION.md for full code-level detail): instead of
hard-replacing with exact truth, hard-replace with truth + spatially- and
vertically-correlated Gaussian noise, calibrated to Ingleby (2017)-style
radiosonde error magnitudes (~0.7-2.0 K by level for temperature, ~1.0-2.2 m/s
by level for wind), with wind noise specifically constructed as a
non-divergent field derived from a random streamfunction (not independent
u/v noise, which would inject unphysical divergence). Horizontal correlation
length ~250 km (Gaussian-smoothed white noise); vertical correlation via
exponential decay in log-pressure space. This is calibrated, physically-
motivated OBSERVATION ERROR, not a weaker/damped correction -- the full-
strength hard-replacement structure (steps 121-240, every 6h) is unchanged;
only the SIGNAL now carries realistic noise instead of being noise-free. This
is a different question from the damped/partial-nudging idea in Section 8.2
below (weaker correction strength) -- that experiment is still open.

Extending the reference implementation's 13-level sigma tables to GraphCast's
37 levels required log-pressure interpolation (documented limitation, flat-
extrapolated above 50 hPa where no reference radiosonde curve exists -- see
perturbation.py's module docstring for the full statement).

A real implementation bug was caught before wasting GPU time: the
perturbation code's local RNG variable was named `rng`, colliding with
chunked_prediction_generator's own JAX PRNGKey parameter of the same name in
the same function scope -- silently corrupting it and crashing the NEXT
chunk's jax.random.split call. A single-chunk unit test missed this (no next
chunk to corrupt); a multi-chunk test with injection starting mid-run caught
it immediately. Fixed (renamed to `noise_rng`), reverified with the same
multi-chunk test, then verified a third way against real GPU output: the
perturbed minus truth RMS difference at 500 hPa matched the calibrated sigma
almost exactly (T: 0.7006 K vs 0.7 K target; U: 1.2000 m/s vs 1.2 m/s
target), with near-zero mean bias -- confirms the noise is unbiased and
correctly calibrated, not just structurally present.

10.1 Result: the T-insertion instability is essentially UNCHANGED by adding
     realistic observation-error noise.

Mature-phase (day 30-60) mean %-RMSE-improvement on the non-inserted fields:

  Experiment              Z            U            V
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  T-Ins  (exact truth)   -199.58%     -195.72%     -212.55%
  T-Ins  (COP-perturbed) -196.87%     -194.56%     -208.94%
  UV-Ins (exact truth)    +52.54%      +99.19%      +99.12%
  UV-Ins (COP-perturbed)  +52.34%      +89.88%      +93.80%

(T's own trivial self-insertion drops from 99.16% (exact) to 86.79%
(perturbed), and UV's from ~99.1-99.2% to ~89.9-93.8% -- both EXPECTED and
CORRECT, since the inserted values are no longer exactly truth by
construction; this is a sanity check that the noise is actually reaching the
inserted field, not a concern.)

The catastrophic T-insertion degradation of Z/U/V, and the healthy
Z-Ins/UV-Ins cross-variable improvement, are both essentially unchanged in
sign and magnitude whether the inserted truth is exact or carries realistic,
calibrated observation error. This rules out "the instability is an artifact
of forcing the model with an unrealistically perfect, noise-free signal" as
an explanation -- adding realistic noise on top of the same full-strength
hard-replacement structure does not meaningfully change either the failure
(T-Ins) or the success (UV-Ins) case. The instability is a property of WHICH
FIELD is being forced and HOW OFTEN/HOW COMPLETELY (Sections 5, 6), not of
how clean the forcing signal is.

10.2 What this does and doesn't tell us.

This result strengthens the case that Section 5's "no learned geostrophic-
adjustment mechanism" explanation is about the STRUCTURE of the forcing
(univariate, hard, high-frequency, high-vertical-resolution) rather than
about the forcing being unrealistically idealized -- a real observing system
feeding realistic, imperfect temperature observations into GraphCast via the
same kind of hard sequential correction would apparently hit the same
instability. It does NOT test the damped/partial-nudging idea (Section 8.2,
still open) -- a WEAKER correction (blending toward truth with some
relaxation coefficient alpha < 1, rather than a full-strength hard
replacement with noisy vs. noise-free truth) remains a genuinely different,
untested variable.

11. LIMITATIONS OF THIS CROSS-MODEL COMPARISON -- READ BEFORE PUBLICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This section exists specifically so nothing here gets overstated in a
manuscript. The four-model comparison in Section 3 is OBSERVATIONAL, not a
controlled ablation: Aurora, FCN, Prithvi, and GraphCast full differ in
every one of the following ways simultaneously, not just in the
architectural properties invoked in Section 5:

  - Truth/training data source: ERA5 for Aurora/FCN/GraphCast, MERRA-2 for
    Prithvi -- a genuinely different reanalysis product, not just a
    different model.
  - Horizontal resolution: 0.25 deg (Aurora, FCN, GraphCast) vs 0.5 deg
    (Prithvi).
  - Native vertical-level count: 13 (Aurora, FCN), 14 (Prithvi), 37
    (GraphCast full) -- see Section 5.3's unresolved status.
  - Insertion cadence: 12h (Aurora, FCN) vs 6h (GraphCast, Prithvi) --
    ruled out as the primary driver in Section 4.2, but still a real
    difference in experimental protocol, not just the model.
  - Initial condition date/season: 1 February 2023 (Aurora, FCN) vs
    1 January 2022 (GraphCast, Prithvi) -- different year AND different
    season. Cross-variable dynamical balance (e.g. the strength of the
    midlatitude jet, baroclinicity, ENSO phase) is season-dependent; this
    report has not checked whether the specific season/state of the
    atmosphere in each model's IC window materially affects how "hard" the
    T-to-wind/mass inversion problem (Section 6) is for that particular
    60-day window, independent of which model is doing the inverting.
  - Training objective and loss weighting: not compared in this report at
    all. Differences in per-variable loss weighting, curriculum
    (single-step vs multi-step autoregressive fine-tuning), and how much
    rollout stability was explicitly optimized for during training could
    each independently affect sensitivity to out-of-distribution forced
    inputs, and have not been examined here.

A rigorous version of this study, suitable for a claim stronger than
"consistent with," would need at least one of: (a) the same model
architecture run at multiple vertical resolutions (Section 8.3), (b) the
same insertion experiment repeated across multiple ICs/seasons for each
model to check season-dependence, or (c) a synthetic/toy dynamical model
where architecture can genuinely be varied while holding data, resolution,
and training procedure fixed. None of these exist yet for this comparison.
The claims in Section 5 should be read, and cited, as the most
parsimonious explanation consistent with the available (confounded)
evidence -- not as an isolated, proven causal mechanism.
